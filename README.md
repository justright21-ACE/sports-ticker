# The FanCave Wire

Full-screen live sports scoreboard, shown on an HP monitor through a Chromecast (and works on TVs and other screens too).

Live page: https://justright21-ace.github.io/sports-ticker/

Data (both free, no API key):
- ESPN scoreboard feed (main source for every league)
- SportsBlaze: backup for NFL, NBA, MLB, WNBA if ESPN can't be reached
- ESPN scoreboard feed: NCAA football, UFC + PFL main card fights for the COMBAT tab (also NCAA men's basketball and NHL), the Premier League and Champions League, and the PGA Tour
- TheSportsDB (free key): major boxing cards on the COMBAT tab (scheduled time and result; no live round-by-round)

Each card also shows the opening spread and over/under (DraftKings, via ESPN) when available.

Options go on the end of the address:
- Favorites are set per league in the CONFIG block (Bears, Eagles, USC, Wisconsin, Spurs, Bulls, White Sox, Blackhawks)
- Every USC or Wisconsin football game gets the primetime-style game-day screen and its own tab, kept 30 minutes after the final (`?gameday=0` turns it off, `?gamedayafter=45` changes the minutes)
- Bears and USC games are must-watch: always a big screen (Bears even on a primetime Sunday), held 5 minutes at a time while live (30 seconds at halftime), with the score, plays and stats re-checked every 10 seconds instead of 30. Animations always finish before a refresh redraws the screen. `?fast=0` goes back to 30-second updates
- One-game NFL nights (a lone Monday or Thursday night game): the game shows only on its primetime screen, and the NFL tab shows the AFC/NFC standings and the NFL league leaders, top 5 in each, on two screens: passing & rushing (passing yards, passing TD, QBR, rushing yards, rushing TD) and receiving & defense (receiving yards, receiving TD, sacks, interceptions). QBR comes from ESPN's QBR table; if that can't be reached, passer rating stands in. The leaders refresh every 30 minutes and right after any NFL game goes final; the heading shows the time they were fetched ("as of 10:52 PM"). Days with more than one game, Thanksgiving included, are unchanged. `?onegame=0` turns it off
- Primetime inactives: in the 3 hours before an SNF/MNF/TNF kickoff, the primetime tab plays a 30-second sequence: a title card with both teams, then each team's logo panel and its inactive players (number, position, name). Uses the official inactive list when ESPN's game feed has it (about 90 minutes before kickoff); before that it shows the players ruled OUT on the injury report, labeled RULED OUT. `?inactives=0` turns it off
- When no primetime game (SNF/MNF/TNF, World Series) is on the board that day, your other teams (Bears, Eagles, Spurs, Bulls, Blackhawks, White Sox) get their own tab and big screen too, with basketball and hockey versions (leaders, team stats, periods, GOAL! banners). `?favscreen=0` turns it off The Eagles (`CONFIG.alwaysScreen`) keep their big screen on primetime days too, like the Bears, but without taking over the way the Bears do.
- The Blackhawks have to earn their own tab and big screen: only once they're above .500 (more wins than regulation losses) or after the NHL All-Star break (the date in `CONFIG.playoffPictureFrom.nhl`), whichever comes first. Until then their games just show with the other NHL games. `?hawksgate=0` turns the rule off
- Injury reports: each team's side panel on the big screens lists who's out / doubtful / questionable (day-to-day in NBA/NHL) before the game and who's out during it (trimmed to fit the screen), and the bottom bar has an INJURIES segment for your teams' games today. Injured reserve is left off. `?injuries=0` turns it off
- League logos in the bottom bar: league logos replace the league names in the bottom bar's labels (e.g. just the NFL logo, or [logo] INJURIES) and next to each headline, on a small light disc so dark logos show. College football and basketball use the NCAA football / basketball logos saved in `img/` plus CFB or CBB (if a file can't load, a drawn football / basketball icon shows instead); COMBAT (UFC + PFL + boxing) keeps its name. Logos come from ESPN like the team logos; if one doesn't load, the league's name shows instead. `?leaguelogos=0` goes back to names only
- League logos in the top bar too: the league tabs show logos instead of names (the primetime tab like MNF stays as text). The COMBAT logo follows what's on: an octagon marked UFC or PFL for MMA, a boxing glove for boxing, a wrestling ring for WWE (live event first, else the next one)
- WWE on the COMBAT tab: premium live events only (Survivor Series, Royal Rumble, WrestleMania, NXT's PLEs…) plus Saturday Night's Main Event, from TheSportsDB like the boxing cards. Weekly shows (Raw, SmackDown, NXT, Main Event, Speed) are skipped
- Any NFL game, and any college football game with a Top 25 team or a team from the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12 or ACC (or Notre Dame), within one score (8 pts) in the 4th quarter or overtime gets its own CLOSE screen, 45 seconds a time. Unranked major-conference games aren't in the Top 25 college feed, so during the season the ticker also checks every FBS game (every 20 min, every 2 min once games are on, every 30 s while one is close); those games only get CLOSE screens, they don't join the NCAAF scores tab. `?closemajor=0` goes back to Top 25 only, `?close=0` turns CLOSE screens off
- Big-screen animations (primetime, game day, CLOSE, World Series): team-color wipe as the screen arrives, the ball glides on the field, red-zone pulse, scoring banners (TOUCHDOWN / FIELD GOAL / SAFETY, lead changes, ties) with a flipping score, a two-minute-warning pulse, a FINAL stamp, and 15+ yard plays (a runner sprinting across, or a ball flying into a pair of hands). A 15+ yard play or a turnover (INTERCEPTION! / FUMBLE! / TURNOVER ON DOWNS, with a loose-ball and possession-swap animation) in a live big-screen game (sacks get their own SACK! animation too). Red zone: game cards glow red with a RED ZONE tag; big screens flash a RED ZONE badge under the team with the ball and sweep a RED ZONE alert across when a team gets inside the 20 (`?redzone=0` off), or any score in a CLOSE game, jumps the rotation straight to that game (a score that breaks a game open keeps its CLOSE screen 3 more minutes). `?motion=0` turns animations off, `?jump=0` turns off the jump
- Red-zone alert slides in and out smoothly and stays solid (it used to blink see-through, so the score flickered behind it); a light sheen glides across instead, and the RED ZONE tags breathe gently rather than blink
- Win celebrations: when the Bears, USC or Wisconsin win, their big screen jumps up at the final and plays a full-screen celebration in team colors after the FINAL stamp (again each time that final screen comes up): BEARS WIN! BEARS WIN! (navy and orange); TROJANS WIN! FIGHT ON! with the Trojan V hand sign pumping on each side (cardinal and gold); BADGERS WIN! ON, WISCONSIN! with the letters jumping around (red and white). Logo spins in, confetti falls, final score underneath; it stays up 15 seconds (confetti and chants keep going the whole time). `?celebratesec=20` changes how long, `?celebrate=0` turns it off
- UP NEXT screen: when nothing's on in the next 24 hours, instead of an empty screen it lists the Bears, USC, Wisconsin, Bulls and Blackhawks: record and standing, last result (W/L and score), and the next game (opponent, day, time, TV) with a countdown, soonest first. From ESPN's team schedules, checked hourly. `?idle=0` turns it off
- Win-probability bar along the bottom of the big screens' score band: away team's color from the left, home team's from the right, with each side's chance to win; it glides as the game swings. Before kickoff it shows ESPN's projection; it's hidden once the game is final. `?winprob=0` turns it off
- Halftime stat battle: at halftime (football and basketball) the big screen's panels give way to a first-half comparison, a bar per stat in team colors with the better number in white: total yards, passing, rushing, 3rd downs, turnovers, time of possession (basketball: FG %, 3-point %, rebounds, assists, turnovers, points in the paint). `?halftime=0` turns it off
- Player headshots (ESPN photos, small and round) on the NFL league leaders, the primetime inactives and the bottom bar's injury lists; a missing photo leaves an empty circle so the names stay lined up. `?headshots=0` turns them off
- Timeouts on the big screens: the three bars under each team are counted from ESPN's play-by-play for the current half (official/TV timeouts don't count). If there's no play-by-play, the scoreboard's numbers are used, except when they say neither team has a timeout left outside the last 3 minutes of a half (ESPN has sent 0/0 by mistake): then the bars are hidden instead of wrong. Hidden at halftime too, when both teams reset to 3
- One-off big screens: `CONFIG.pinGames` gives a game its own tab and primetime-style screen (90 s, back after every other screen while it's on) on one date, e.g. `{ date: '2026-11-07', lg: 'ncaaf', teams: ['ND', 'MIA'], tab: 'ND·MIA' }`
- College football rivalries: about 60 named rivalry and trophy games are built in (The Game, Iron Bowl, Holy War, Catholics vs. Convicts, Red River, Crosstown Showdown and the Victory Bell, Paul Bunyan's Axe, Old Oaken Bucket, Little Brown Jug, Apple Cup...). Their score cards get a gold border, a faded trophy across the card, and the line under the scores takes turns between "RIVALRY · name" and "FOR · trophy" ("WIS WINS · Paul Bunyan's Axe" after the final). On a big screen the name is in the heading, and before kickoff / after the final the middle panel shows the trophy and who takes it home. Trophy pictures: your own photos first (put them in `img/trophies/` and list them in `CONFIG.trophyImages`), else a photo from the trophy's own Wikipedia article (looked up by the screen, kept 30 days; only the trophy's own article, never a logo), else a drawn icon (cup, axe, bucket, bell, jug, cannon, hat, boot, egg, shillelagh, crossed swords). All-time series record ("Michigan leads 62–51–6"): from the rivalry's Wikipedia page (the series line in its info box, kept a week); it takes a turn on the card ("SERIES · MICH leads 62–51–6") and shows on the big screen (in the trophy box before kickoff / after the final, under the score table during the game). `?rivalry=0` turns it all off, `?trophyphoto=0` keeps the drawn icons, `?series=0` hides the records
- NFL upsets: a team favored by more than 2.5 points (the closing betting line, else the opening one) that's trailing at halftime or later gets the same UPSET screen: its own tab, 90 seconds a time, the UPSET ALERT banner ("LIONS (-6.5) TRAIL THE PACKERS BY 4"), and 15 minutes after the final if it holds. Primetime and your teams' games stay on their own screens. ESPN's scoreboard drops the line at kickoff, so a screen that came on mid-game looks the line up in that game's ESPN summary instead. `?nflupset=0` turns it off
- Highlight photo on the big screen: when ESPN posts a new clip for a football game that's on a big screen (primetime, your teams, close games, upsets), the middle panel swaps the field graphic for the clip's photo with its headline ("Josh Allen 22-yard TD run") for 15 seconds, then the field comes back. The score, both leaders panels and the quarter table stay up. Just a picture, so it works in any browser (BrowseHere too). Clips already posted when the screen first sees the game, or older than 12 hours, are skipped. `?panelphoto=0` turns it off
- Highlights: **paused** (Nov 8 2026) until a better TV browser is found; `?highlights=1` turns them on for a screen. Notes: froze on an iPad, plays on a phone / Chrome, BrowseHere pops videos out. The clip stays invisible until the video is actually moving, so the first-second stutter isn't seen. Some browsers won't start video by themselves until the page has been tapped / clicked once; then a small "HIGHLIGHTS tap or click the screen once" note shows, the screens keep rotating, and one tap lets that clip (and every later one, until the page reloads) play on its own. How it works: when ESPN posts a new highlight clip for a Bears, Eagles, USC, Wisconsin or primetime NFL game, it plays full screen with a HIGHLIGHT bar (teams + the clip's title), muted unless sound is on, up to 90 seconds; the rotation waits and carries on after. Clips play from kickoff until 12 hours after the game ends (it keeps checking the game's page for recaps after the final, every 3 minutes), and a clip posted more than 12 hours ago never plays. Clips already posted when the screen first sees a game are skipped. Only the newest 3 wait in line; a clip that won't play is skipped. Picture cards instead of video: BrowseHere (TCL / Google TV) opens every video in its own pop-up player, so by default a new clip shows as a full-screen card for 10 seconds instead: the clip's photo, HIGHLIGHT, its headline, and the teams with the score; `?hlvideo=1` plays the video instead (phones, Chrome). `?hd=1` uses the 720p files. `?hltest=1` plays the newest clip right after the page loads (your teams' games first, else any NFL / college game today), once, to see how it looks. ESPN posts clips a few minutes after the play, not every game has them, and ESPN can change or block this feed
- Sound (off unless the address has `?sound=1`, so you choose which screen makes noise): a two-tone horn when a game newly goes on UPSET ALERT, a chime for a score in a close football game, a fanfare for a Bears touchdown, and a longer fanfare when any of your teams wins. Browsers only allow sound after someone clicks, taps or presses a key on the page (a TV remote's OK works), once each time the page loads, so a small "SOUND click or press OK" note shows until then. `?volume=` 0-100 (70), quiet from 11pm to 8am (`?quiet=23-8`, `?quiet=0` never quiet), never two sounds within 6 seconds
- The field (football big screens): an angled field with alternating stripes, end zones in team colors, goalposts and yard numbers; a pin over the ball on the blue line of scrimmage, the yellow line to gain, and the last play drawn as an arc from where it started (black dot) to where it ended (a dot in the offense's color), drawing itself in. Above it a CURRENT DRIVE row: the offense's logo, Down, Start, Ball On, Plays. Drawn as one picture so it looks the same in every browser. `?field3d=0` brings back the flat field with the big down & distance
- Replay reviews: when a review reverses something that matters (a touchdown, interception, fumble, catch, a spot / first down) a red OVERTURNED banner with a replay monitor shows what changed ("TOUCHDOWN OVERTURNED · BOOTH REVIEW", "INTERCEPTION OVERTURNED · CHALLENGED BY BEARS"); a coach's challenge that fails shows CALL STANDS ("LIONS LOSE A TIMEOUT"); a booth review that just confirms the call shows nothing. A score that goes back down also gets OVERTURNED. The reversed call doesn't set off its own banner (no INTERCEPTION for a pick that was overturned), and LAST PLAY shows what stood ("After review: …"). `?reviews=0` turns it off
- Injury stoppages: while the newest play says a player was hurt ("J.Johnson was injured during the play"), the game shows a white INJURY tag with a red cross until the next snap. A timeout called during the stoppage doesn't end it. On a score card the tag takes the place of LIVE, and a small cross sits next to the injured player's team. On a big screen the tag sits under that team: "INJURY · JAYLON JOHNSON". If the team can't be told, it sits next to the clock instead. Mostly football: the basketball and hockey feeds rarely mention injuries. `?injury=0` turns it off
- Scores not updating: if nothing has refreshed for 10 minutes (Wi-Fi down, ESPN not answering, or the refresh got stuck), a yellow SCORES NOT UPDATING bar covers the bottom bar with the time of the last good update. It goes away by itself on the next good refresh. `?stale=0` turns it off, `?stale=15` changes the minutes
- New versions load by themselves: every 5 minutes the ticker checks for a new version of itself. Once the new version has shown up twice in a row, it reloads at the next screen change (never in the middle of a banner). A screen whose scores have been stuck for 15 minutes, while the page itself can still be reached, also gets a fresh start (at most once every 30 minutes). `?autoreload=0` turns it off
- Breaking news for your teams (Bears, Eagles, USC, Wisconsin, Bulls, Spurs, White Sox, Blackhawks): every 10 minutes it checks ESPN's news for each team. A story from the last 3 hours about a trade, signing, release, waiver, big injury (torn ACL, out for the season, injured reserve, surgery), suspension, transfer portal move, or a coach fired, hired or resigning slides a BREAKING banner over the top of the screen for 20 seconds: the team in its colors, the time it was posted and the headline. Fantasy, odds, previews, takeaways and other filler are skipped. Each story shows once, even after a reload. `?breaking=0` turns it off
- Bowl season: every bowl game is on the NCAAF scores screens (not just ranked teams'), labeled with its bowl ("POP-TARTS BOWL", "ROSE · CFP QTR"). College Football Playoff games get a CFP big screen (90 s while live, back after every other screen). New Year's Six bowls and bowls with a Top 25 team get a BOWLS big screen (45 s). The heading carries the full name: "NCAAF · ROSE BOWL · CFP QUARTERFINAL". USC and Wisconsin bowls keep their own game-day screens. On a live card the round or bowl tag uses its short form, so it doesn't run into the clock; squarer screens leave it off live cards. `?bowls=0` turns it off
- COMBAT icons follow the card on screen: on a night with both a UFC and a PFL card, the top-bar octagon says UFC while the UFC card is up and PFL while the PFL card is up (it used to show whichever event started first, so a UFC card could sit under a PFL icon). The bottom bar gives each promotion (and boxing) its own label. Each feed also keeps only its own promotion's events, in case ESPN's PFL list ever includes a UFC card
- COMBAT screens are titled with the event itself: "UFC 334" over "Gane vs. Hokit · Main card · Tonight", "PFL DUBAI" over "Nemkov vs. Bilostenniy · …", a boxing card by its fight ("CANELO VS MAYWEATHER", last names when the full names run long; Canelo by his one name), a WWE event by its name. The bottom bar labels each event the same way, next to its icon
- Fighter photos and flags on COMBAT cards: each fighter's photo sits in the circle next to his name, with his country's flag on its corner. UFC and PFL fighters use ESPN's headshots and flags; boxers are looked up on TheSportsDB (photo and nationality, a couple of names per refresh, remembered for 30 days), with flags from flagcdn.com. No photo: just the flag; neither: the name alone. `?fighterpics=0` turns it off
- NFL power rankings: a screen in the NFL rotation (25 s) ranking all 32 teams by ESPN's Football Power Index, ESPN's numbers-based power ranking, which updates after every game. ESPN's weekly written power rankings aren't in a data feed. Each row has the team's record, its FPI rating (how many points better or worse than an average team on a neutral field), its move since about a week earlier, and its chance to make the playoffs (IN / OUT once clinched or eliminated). The Bears and Eagles rows are highlighted. The +/- column fills in after the ticker has seen a week of rankings. `?power=0` turns it off
- Soccer: the Premier League (EPL tab) and the Champions League (UCL tab) from ESPN. Score cards with the live minute ("67'"), HT / FT, and each club's W-D-L record; both are in the bottom bar. The EPL tab ends with the Premier League table (20 clubs: played, won, drawn, lost, goal difference, points; lines under the top 4 and above the bottom 3), August to May. A soccer tab only shows while it has a game in the next day (EPL: or the table in season). `?epltable=0` turns the table off
- Golf (PGA Tour, from ESPN): while a tournament is on (from the morning of round 1 to the morning after the final round) a GOLF tab shows the leaderboard, top 20: place (T for ties), player with flag, total to par (under par in red), today's round and holes played; the leaders are in the bottom bar too, labeled with the tournament's name in the corner (a major's full name), always without the sponsor's "presented by ..." ("Arnold Palmer Invitational presented by Mastercard" shows as ARNOLD PALMER INVITATIONAL, here and on the leaderboard screen). The majors (Masters, PGA Championship, U.S. Open, The Open) and the Ryder Cup take over the tab's name (MASTERS, PGA CHAMP, U.S. OPEN, THE OPEN, RYDER CUP), sit up front with the big games, and while play is on come back after every other screen. `?golf=0` turns it off
- Part-year leagues come and go by month (`CONFIG.leagueMonths`): men's college basketball November to April, the WNBA May to October
- WNBA: during the regular season there's no WNBA tab, cards or bottom bar; a game within 5 points in the last 5 minutes of the 4th quarter or in overtime gets a CLOSE big screen (25 s, like the NBA ones, no TV rule). Once the playoffs start the whole league shows as usual. `?wnba=all` shows the regular season too
- NFL special games: any NFL game that isn't part of the Sunday afternoon slate gets the primetime treatment (its own tab and big screen, game-day pregame, inactives, 90 s while live): Sunday night, every Monday game, Thursday night, Thursday afternoon, and any Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday or Saturday game. Holidays get their own names: THANKSGIVING (all three Thanksgiving games share the tab: "Thanksgiving Football", the night game "Thanksgiving Night Football"), Thanksgiving Eve (WED), BLACK FRIDAY, CHRISTMAS (any day) and Christmas Eve. Late-season Saturday games show as SAT ("Saturday Football"). A Bears game is still must-watch (5 minutes a time). `?specialdays=0` goes back to just SNF / MNF / TNF
- Scoring package (on; `?tdfx=0` turns it off) for touchdowns, field goals and safeties (extra points and 2-point tries just flip the number; a 2-point try is no longer called a safety): on the big screen, the scoring team's animal (or helmet) leaps up out of the bottom, two helmets slide in from the sides, TOUCHDOWN drops in letter by letter in the team's colors over a glow, then the scorer's photo rises behind the word with the team logo on one side and his name on the other; about 7 seconds, in place of the flat banner
- Animal photos: the touchdown run and package use real cut-out photos of each animal (img/td/, about 570 KB in all; `?tdphotos=0` goes back to the drawings). Texans get a bull. Teams without an animal get their helmet
- College animals: the run and the scoring package use cut-out photos for college teams too, matched by the team's name (TD_COLLEGE: school-specific first, e.g. Tennessee → coonhound, Miami → ibis, SMU → pony, Arkansas → warthog, Texas A&M → steer; then the nickname, e.g. Bulldogs, Tigers, Eagles, Wildcats → bobcat). Schools without one (USC, Alabama, Ohio State, Florida…) get their helmet with the logo
- College figures: schools named for people or things get those too (Trojan, Spartan, knights, Viking, leprechaun, Cavalier, pirate, cowboy, Mountaineer, Sooner wagon, Commodore, Blue Devil, Sun Devil, Boilermaker train, cyclone, Green Wave / Crimson Tide wave …)
- College hoops tournaments (March 1 - April 12, `?tourney=0` off): the big-five conference championship games (ACC, Big East, Big Ten, Big 12, SEC) and every NCAA tournament game (First Four to the title game; not the NIT / women's / semifinals) get a big screen (MADNESS / FINAL FOUR / TITLE GAME / "ACC FINAL" tabs) and the winner's celebration: "DUKE WINS!" with the round ("SWEET 16 BOUND", "BIG TEN CHAMPIONS", "NATIONAL CHAMPIONS"), the school's animal or figure in the corners. The TV jumps to each winner once
- Holidays (`?holiday=0` off, `?holiday=juneteenth` / christmas / july4 / newyear / halloween / thanksgiving / bhm ... previews): New Year's Eve & Day, MLK Day, Black History Month (all February), Valentine's Day (hearts; takes Feb 14), Presidents' Day, St. Patrick's, Easter, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, July 4th, Labor Day, Halloween, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve & Day. A strip in the holiday's colors under the header, its name by the clock, a tint behind everything, things drifting at the edges (snow, leaves, bats, shamrocks, fireworks, stars), and a ~7 s greeting card every 15-60 min (never over a live big game or a celebration). Juneteenth: red-black-green strip, the Juneteenth flag's star and burst, fireworks, "FREEDOM DAY · JUNE 19, 1865"
- Clock check (football): ESPN's scoreboard clock sometimes jumps ahead (0:31 when it's 9:31). If it's more than ~1.5 min of game clock past the last snap in the play-by-play, the big screen and crawl use the play-by-play feed's clock, or the last snap's (`?clockcheck=0` off)
- Crunch time (every big screen: primetime, close games, upset alerts): the last 2:00 of a half / the game. Football and NBA: 2nd quarter, 4th, OT; college hoops: each half and OT; NHL: the 3rd period and OT. With only one live big-screen game the TV goes to it right away and stays until halftime / the final; with several, the normal rotation runs but a game in its last 2:00 holds 5 minutes like the Bears (`?crunch=0` off)
- NFL top performers (`?topperf=0` off): when the Sunday night game ends, a page in the NFL rotation shows the week's leaders from every finished game's box score (Thursday on): passing, rushing and receiving yards, receptions, touchdowns, tackles, interceptions, top 3 each with the leader's photo. It updates when Monday night ends and stays 24 hours after that; the next week starts over
- Red zone: the box, field glow, panels and RED ZONE alert use a checked red zone (the ball really inside the other 20, and nothing left over from a score until the next snap / kickoff), not ESPN's flag alone
- Big-screen leaders (football): each team's three best games so far, any position, scored roughly like fantasy points (yards, TDs, catches; tackles, sacks, takeaways; INTs thrown count against), with a stat line for what made the game (a QB's rushing, a back's catches when they add up). Before kickoff: season leaders as before
- Broadcast badges (`?badges=0` off): the NFL primetime / special tabs show a badge instead of the letters (img/badge/): Sunday, Monday and Thursday Night Football, Thanksgiving, Black Friday, Christmas (Day and Eve), International (a Sunday-morning kickoff, now its own special screen), and Kickoff & Specials (the season opener and the Tuesday / Wednesday / Friday / Saturday games)
- Badge watermark: the game's broadcast badge sits faintly across the NFL primetime / special screen (`?bgbadge=0` off, =2 stronger, =side)
- Time-left line (`?pbar=0` off): the rule over the bottom bar drains as the screen's time runs out. Holds of 45 s or less show it the whole time; longer holds (Bears, a game's last 2:00) only for the last 30 s; a screen locked in crunch time shows none
- Matchup view: [matchup.html](https://justright21-ace.github.io/sports-ticker/matchup.html) (= `index.html?view=matchup`) shows one football game full screen: the real helmets facing each other, the score, the clock and the field with the ball, plus every big-screen animation (touchdown / field goal / safety package, red zone, big plays, turnovers, sacks, penalties, reviews, the win). Shows the featured games: NFL primetime / special games (SNF, MNF, TNF, holidays, international...), the Bears, USC, Wisconsin, and the Saturday-night college game on ABC. Two or more on at once: 90 s each, and a game in its last 2:00 of a half takes the screen until halftime / the final (then 25 s for the result). Between games: one that just ended (45 min), else the next one with its kickoff time. With no featured football game on (or in the off-season): the game of the day from the other sports, picked once when the day's scores are in and then locked: (1) one of your teams in the postseason, (2) a title game (NBA Finals, Stanley Cup Final, World Series, Final Four / title game, a cup final), (3) one of your teams (Bulls, Spurs, Blackhawks, White Sox; USC / Wisconsin hoops), (4) any other playoff / tournament game, (5) the best matchup (ranked vs ranked, best records, national TV). Ties go to the sport ranked higher: `?sportrank=nba,ncaab,mlb,nhl,ucl,epl` (default NFL, college football, NBA, college hoops, MLB, NHL, Champions League, Premier League; a sport left out is never picked). When it's over (45 min after the final) the next one on the day's list takes over. `?gotdpin=duke` makes a team's game today's pick, `?gotd=0` goes back to the next football game. Logos in place of helmets; baseball gets the at-bat view, and after each ball in play a hit tracker until the next pitch: the ball's flight on an angled ballpark (seen from high behind home plate; a generic 330 / 400 ft park), where it landed, gold for a homer / green for a hit / red for an out, with exit velocity, launch angle, distance and MLB's description (`?mlbhit=0` off); basketball (NBA, college) gets the quarter-by-quarter (or half) line score under the clock, timeouts left and team fouls / BONUS under each team, the two top scorers a team with headshots, team stat bars (FG%, 3PT%, rebounds, turnovers, assists), and a full court with the last shot (the ball where it was taken, a line to the rim, the shooter, made or missed; free throws on the line, "free throw 1 of 2") and the shots before it: each team shoots at the basket on its logo's side. Who has the ball shows as a BALL tag by the team and an arrow on the court (worked out from the last play: a make, rebound or turnover). From ESPN's play-by-play (`?bbpanel=0` off). Every sport shows each team's record (or the playoff series) under its name. `?team=CHI` / `?team=wisconsin` pins a team instead
- Game-time weather (`?weather=0` off): outdoor games only (NFL, college football, MLB, soccer; never basketball, hockey or fights, never a dome or a usually-closed roof). During the game the conditions there now, before it the forecast for kickoff / first pitch with the rain or snow chance: an icon, temperature, conditions and wind.
