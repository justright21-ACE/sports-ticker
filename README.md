# The FanCave Wire

Full-screen live sports scoreboard, shown on an HP monitor through a Chromecast (and works on TVs and other screens too).

Live page: https://justright21-ace.github.io/sports-ticker/

Data (both free, no API key):
- ESPN scoreboard feed (main source for every league)
- SportsBlaze: backup for NFL, NBA, MLB, WNBA if ESPN can't be reached
- ESPN scoreboard feed: NCAA football, UFC + PFL main card fights for the COMBAT tab (also NCAA men's basketball and NHL)
- TheSportsDB (free key): major boxing cards on the COMBAT tab (scheduled time and result; no live round-by-round)

Each card also shows the opening spread and over/under (DraftKings, via ESPN) when available.

Options go on the end of the address:
- Favorites are set per league in the CONFIG block (Bears, Eagles, USC, Wisconsin, Spurs, Bulls, White Sox, Blackhawks)
- Every USC or Wisconsin football game gets the primetime-style game-day screen and its own tab, kept 30 minutes after the final (`?gameday=0` turns it off, `?gamedayafter=45` changes the minutes)
- Bears and USC games are must-watch: always a big screen (Bears even on a primetime Sunday), held 5 minutes at a time while live (30 seconds at halftime), with the score, plays and stats re-checked every 10 seconds instead of 30. Animations always finish before a refresh redraws the screen. `?fast=0` goes back to 30-second updates
- One-game NFL nights (a lone Monday or Thursday night game): the game shows only on its primetime screen, and the NFL tab shows the AFC/NFC standings and the NFL league leaders, top 5 in each, on two screens: passing & rushing (passing yards, passing TD, QBR, rushing yards, rushing TD) and receiving & defense (receiving yards, receiving TD, sacks, interceptions). QBR comes from ESPN's QBR table; if that can't be reached, passer rating stands in. The leaders refresh every 30 minutes and right after any NFL game goes final; the heading shows the time they were fetched ("as of 10:52 PM"). Days with more than one game, Thanksgiving included, are unchanged. `?onegame=0` turns it off
- Primetime inactives: in the 3 hours before an SNF/MNF/TNF kickoff, the primetime tab plays a 30-second sequence: a title card with both teams, then each team's logo panel and its inactive players (number, position, name). Uses the official inactive list when ESPN's game feed has it (about 90 minutes before kickoff); before that it shows the players ruled OUT on the injury report, labeled RULED OUT. `?inactives=0` turns it off
- When no primetime game (SNF/MNF/TNF, World Series) is on the board that day, your other teams (Bears, Eagles, Spurs, Bulls, Blackhawks, White Sox) get their own tab and big screen too, with basketball and hockey versions (leaders, team stats, periods, GOAL! banners). `?favscreen=0` turns it off
- The Blackhawks have to earn their own tab and big screen: only once they're above .500 (more wins than regulation losses) or after the NHL All-Star break (the date in `CONFIG.playoffPictureFrom.nhl`), whichever comes first. Until then their games just show with the other NHL games. `?hawksgate=0` turns the rule off
- Injury reports: each team's side panel on the big screens lists who's out / doubtful / questionable (day-to-day in NBA/NHL) before the game and who's out during it (trimmed to fit the screen), and the bottom bar has an INJURIES segment for your teams' games today. Injured reserve is left off. `?injuries=0` turns it off
- League logos in the bottom bar: league logos replace the league names in the bottom bar's labels (e.g. just the NFL logo, or [logo] INJURIES) and next to each headline, on a small light disc so dark logos show. College football and basketball use the NCAA football / basketball logos saved in `img/` plus CFB or CBB (if a file can't load, a drawn football / basketball icon shows instead); COMBAT (UFC + PFL + boxing) keeps its name. Logos come from ESPN like the team logos; if one doesn't load, the league's name shows instead. `?leaguelogos=0` goes back to names only
- League logos in the top bar too: the league tabs show logos instead of names (the primetime tab like MNF stays as text). The COMBAT logo follows what's on: an octagon marked UFC or PFL for MMA, a boxing glove for boxing, a wrestling ring for WWE (live event first, else the next one)
- WWE on the COMBAT tab: premium live events only (Survivor Series, Royal Rumble, WrestleMania, NXT's PLEs…) plus Saturday Night's Main Event, from TheSportsDB like the boxing cards. Weekly shows (Raw, SmackDown, NXT, Main Event, Speed) are skipped
- Any NFL game, and any college football game with a Top 25 team or a team from the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12 or ACC (or Notre Dame), within one score (8 pts) in the 4th quarter or overtime gets its own CLOSE screen, 45 seconds a time. Unranked major-conference games aren't in the Top 25 college feed, so during the season the ticker also checks every FBS game (every 20 min, every 2 min once games are on, every 30 s while one is close); those games only get CLOSE screens, they don't join the NCAAF scores tab. `?closemajor=0` goes back to Top 25 only, `?close=0` turns CLOSE screens off
- Big-screen animations (primetime, game day, CLOSE, World Series): team-color wipe as the screen arrives, the ball glides on the field, red-zone pulse, scoring banners (TOUCHDOWN / FIELD GOAL / SAFETY, lead changes, ties) with a flipping score, a two-minute-warning pulse, a FINAL stamp, and 20+ yard plays (a runner sprinting across, or a ball flying into a pair of hands). A 20+ yard play or a turnover (INTERCEPTION! / FUMBLE! / TURNOVER ON DOWNS, with a loose-ball and possession-swap animation) in a live big-screen game (sacks get their own SACK! animation too). Red zone: game cards glow red with a RED ZONE tag; big screens flash a RED ZONE badge under the team with the ball and sweep a RED ZONE alert across when a team gets inside the 20 (`?redzone=0` off), or any score in a CLOSE game, jumps the rotation straight to that game (a score that breaks a game open keeps its CLOSE screen 3 more minutes). `?motion=0` turns animations off, `?jump=0` turns off the jump
- Red-zone alert slides in and out smoothly and stays solid (it used to blink see-through, so the score flickered behind it); a light sheen glides across instead, and the RED ZONE tags breathe gently rather than blink
- Win celebrations: when the Bears, USC or Wisconsin win, their big screen jumps up at the final and plays a full-screen celebration in team colors after the FINAL stamp (again each time that final screen comes up): BEARS WIN! BEARS WIN! (navy and orange); TROJANS WIN! FIGHT ON! with the Trojan V hand sign pumping on each side (cardinal and gold); BADGERS WIN! ON, WISCONSIN! with the letters jumping around (red and white). Logo spins in, confetti falls, final score underneath; it stays up 15 seconds (confetti and chants keep going the whole time). `?celebratesec=20` changes how long, `?celebrate=0` turns it off
- UP NEXT screen: when nothing's on in the next 24 hours, instead of an empty screen it lists the Bears, USC, Wisconsin, Bulls and Blackhawks: record and standing, last result (W/L and score), and the next game (opponent, day, time, TV) with a countdown, soonest first. From ESPN's team schedules, checked hourly. `?idle=0` turns it off
- Win-probability bar along the bottom of the big screens' score band: away team's color from the left, home team's from the right, with each side's chance to win; it glides as the game swings. Before kickoff it shows ESPN's projection; it's hidden once the game is final. `?winprob=0` turns it off
- Halftime stat battle: at halftime (football and basketball) the big screen's panels give way to a first-half comparison, a bar per stat in team colors with the better number in white: total yards, passing, rushing, 3rd downs, turnovers, time of possession (basketball: FG %, 3-point %, rebounds, assists, turnovers, points in the paint). `?halftime=0` turns it off
- Player headshots (ESPN photos, small and round) on the NFL league leaders, the primetime inactives and the bottom bar's injury lists; a missing photo leaves an empty circle so the names stay lined up. `?headshots=0` turns them off
- Timeouts on the big screens: the three bars under each team are counted from ESPN's play-by-play for the current half (official/TV timeouts don't count). If there's no play-by-play, the scoreboard's numbers are used, except when they say neither team has a timeout left outside the last 3 minutes of a half (ESPN has sent 0/0 by mistake): then the bars are hidden instead of wrong. Hidden at halftime too, when both teams reset to 3
- One-off big screens: `CONFIG.pinGames` gives a game its own tab and primetime-style screen (90 s, back after every other screen while it's on) on one date, e.g. `{ date: '2026-11-07', lg: 'ncaaf', teams: ['ND', 'MIA'], tab: 'ND·MIA' }`
- College football rivalries: about 60 named rivalry and trophy games are built in (The Game, Iron Bowl, Holy War, Catholics vs. Convicts, Red River, Crosstown Showdown and the Victory Bell, Paul Bunyan's Axe, Old Oaken Bucket, Little Brown Jug, Apple Cup...). Their score cards get a gold border, a faded trophy across the card, and the line under the scores takes turns between "RIVALRY · name" and "FOR · trophy" ("WIS WINS · Paul Bunyan's Axe" after the final). On a big screen the name is in the heading, and before kickoff / after the final the middle panel shows the trophy and who takes it home. Trophy pictures: your own photos first (put them in `img/trophies/` and list them in `CONFIG.trophyImages`), else a photo from the trophy's own Wikipedia article (looked up by the screen, kept 30 days; only the trophy's own article, never a logo), else a drawn icon (cup, axe, bucket, bell, jug, cannon, hat, boot, egg, shillelagh, crossed swords). All-time series record ("Michigan leads 62–51–6"): from the rivalry's Wikipedia page (the series line in its info box, kept a week); it takes a turn on the card ("SERIES · MICH leads 62–51–6") and shows on the big screen (in the trophy box before kickoff / after the final, under the score table during the game). `?rivalry=0` turns it all off, `?trophyphoto=0` keeps the drawn icons, `?series=0` hides the records
- NFL upsets: a team favored by more than 2.5 points (the closing betting line, else the opening one) that's trailing at halftime or later gets the same UPSET screen: its own tab, 90 seconds a time, the UPSET ALERT banner ("LIONS (-6.5) TRAIL THE PACKERS BY 4"), and 15 minutes after the final if it holds. Primetime and your teams' games stay on their own screens. ESPN's scoreboard drops the line at kickoff, so a screen that came on mid-game looks the line up in that game's ESPN summary instead. `?nflupset=0` turns it off
- Highlights (off unless the address has `?highlights=1`): when ESPN posts a new highlight clip for a Bears, USC, Wisconsin or primetime NFL game, it plays full screen with a HIGHLIGHT bar (teams + the clip's title), muted unless sound is on, up to 90 seconds; the rotation waits and carries on after. Clips play from kickoff until 12 hours after the game ends (it keeps checking the game's page for recaps after the final, every 3 minutes), and a clip posted more than 12 hours ago never plays. Clips already posted when the screen first sees a game are skipped. Only the newest 3 wait in line; a clip that won't play is skipped. `?hd=1` uses the 720p files. ESPN posts clips a few minutes after the play, not every game has them, and ESPN can change or block this feed
- Sound (off unless the address has `?sound=1`, so you choose which screen makes noise): a two-tone horn when a game newly goes on UPSET ALERT, a chime for a score in a close football game, a fanfare for a Bears touchdown, and a longer fanfare when any of your teams wins. Browsers only allow sound after someone clicks, taps or presses a key on the page (a TV remote's OK works), once each time the page loads, so a small "SOUND click or press OK" note shows until then. `?volume=` 0-100 (70), quiet from 11pm to 8am (`?quiet=23-8`, `?quiet=0` never quiet), never two sounds within 6 seconds
- UPSET ALERT: a ranked college football team losing in the 2nd half (3rd quarter on) to an unranked or lower-ranked team, by any margin, gets the primetime treatment: its own big screen (tab UPSET), 90 seconds a time, back after every other screen while it's on (`?upsetsec=` to change). The screen jumps to it the moment it happens and a yellow-and-black UPSET ALERT banner sweeps across ("#2 OHIO STATE TRAILS PENN STATE BY 3"). If the upset holds, it stays in the rotation 15 minutes after the final as UPSET FINAL ("#5 OREGON FALLS TO WASHINGTON"). `?upset=0` turns it off

- CFP bracket: once the College Football Playoff rankings are out, the NCAAF tab opens with a 12-team bracket built from them ("if the season ended today"), then the Top 25. It follows the 2026-27 rules: the Big Ten, SEC, Big 12 and ACC champions plus the highest-ranked team from the American, C-USA, MAC, Mountain West, Pac-12 and Sun Belt get in automatically (before title games, each conference's highest-ranked team stands in as its champion, marked ★); Notre Dame is in if it's top 12; the rest are the highest-ranked teams left. Seeds go straight by ranking, top 4 get byes, and an automatic qualifier ranked below 12 takes the last seed (shown with its real rank, e.g. "Boise State #18"), bumping the 12th team (listed under the bracket). Drawn as TV-style score bars: each team in its own color with the seed in a white block; your teams get a gold outline. The CFP logo sits next to the heading and a faded CFP trophy with a gold glow sits behind the title game (your images in `img/cfp-logo.png` and `img/cfp-trophy.png`; if either is missing it just isn't shown). First round 12 at 5, 11 at 6, 10 at 7, 9 at 8; quarterfinals 1 v 8/9, 4 v 5/12, 2 v 7/10, 3 v 6/11. Conferences come from ESPN's FBS standings, checked twice a day. `?cfpbracket=0` turns it off
- College basketball close games: a game with a Top 25 team or a team from the ACC, Big East, Big Ten, Big 12 or SEC, within 5 points in the last 5 minutes or overtime, gets a CLOSE big screen. Basketball moves fast, so it stays up 25 seconds a time (upsets 45). Unranked major-conference games aren't in the Top 25 feed, so in season the ticker also checks those five conferences' games (every 20 min, every 2 min once games are on, every 30 s while one is close); they only get CLOSE screens, not the scores tab. `?cbbclose=0` turns it off, `?cbbsec=` changes the time
- College basketball upset watch: in the 2nd half (or overtime), a ranked team trailing an unranked team, by any margin, gets an UPSET screen like football's, but 45 seconds a time since the game moves faster (15 minutes after the final); the display jumps to it the first time it happens, with an UPSET ALERT banner ("#3 KENTUCKY TRAILS FURMAN BY 2"). Two halves in the score table, "2ND HALF · 6:02" on the clock. `?cbbupset=0` turns it off
- Big screens shrink 3-digit basketball scores so "106 – 109" and the FINAL stamp never collide
- Favorite teams are held first (and on every screen of their league) only on game day, from that morning until the final; after that their game sorts in with the other finals, and on other days they're treated like any other game
- LINES screens: college football and basketball games that haven't started show sportsbook-style instead of score cards: start time, TV and stadium, then away over home with rank and record, and three boxes each: SPREAD (with its price), MONEY, TOTAL (O/U with its price). 4 games per screen; at kickoff a game goes back to a live card. Lines are DraftKings', from ESPN. `?lines=0` turns it off
- Playoff Picture: after a league's standings, one more screen shows who'd be in "if the season ended today", both conferences side by side, with the cut lines marked and the teams chasing shown with games back. NFL from week 9: 4 division leaders (seeds 1-4) and 3 wild cards, then in the hunt. NBA after the All-Star break: seeds 1-6, play-in 7-10. NHL after its All-Star break: the top 3 in each division plus 2 wild cards, with points. MLB after the All-Star break: 3 division winners and 3 wild cards. WNBA after its All-Star break: the top 8. Regular season only; ESPN's own seeds (with its tiebreakers) are used when the standings include them, and clinched teams get their x / y / z mark. The All-Star dates are in `CONFIG.playoffPictureFrom`. `?picture=0` turns it off
- Standings follow the season: they show from the day before opening day until the season is over (champion crowned or the playoffs' end date passed), and a finished league's tab drops off the top bar. MLB also shows spring training standings (Cactus and Grapefruit League) once camp games begin, about five weeks before opening day. Opening day and the playoff end come from ESPN's season calendar (checked twice a day); if that can't be reached, the old rule applies (hidden once a champion is seen)
- NBA Cup: from the first Cup night until the day after the championship, the NBA standings rotation adds an NBA CUP screen. Group stage: all six groups (W-L and point differential from the Cup games, ties broken by head-to-head, point differential, points), with the group leaders and each conference's wild card marked. Knockout rounds: the bracket with scores (quarterfinals, semifinals, the final), the winner tagged CUP CHAMPIONS. Then it goes away. The dates and groups are set each season in `CONFIG.nbaCup` (2026-27 filled in). `?nbacup=0` turns it off
- Big screens for games other than football (Bulls, Spurs, Blackhawks, White Sox) don't come up until 4 hours before the start; football keeps its all-day game-day screen, and so do championship series: every World Series, NBA Finals, Stanley Cup Final and WNBA Finals game gets a big screen all game day, favorite team or not (`CONFIG.bigScreenLeadHrs`)
- Top-bar order: after the big-screen tabs (primetime, your teams, close games), the league tabs go in the order their games are on: a league with a game on now first, then whichever league's next game starts soonest; leagues with nothing left today go last in their usual order. The screens rotate in the same order
## Ideas for later
- Screen care for older monitors: shift the header, bottom bar and its label a few pixels every few minutes and dim the screen overnight when nothing is live, to avoid burn-in
- A league drops off the top bar once its season is over (champion decided or ESPN says off-season), after the title game result has been up 12 hours; it comes back on its own next season (`?ended=1` keeps every tab) If the screen wasn't on for the clinching game (or ESPN's calendar still says postseason), it looks back over the last 30 days of playoff games every 6 hours until it finds the champion.
- `?fav=DAL,TEX` override favorites for every league
- `?leagues=nfl,ncaaf,nba` choose leagues (nfl, ncaaf, nba, mlb, wnba, ncaab, nhl)
- NHL tab with the Blackhawks as the favorite
- College football Top 25 screen switches to the College Football Playoff rankings once they are released (AP moves to the second column); ranks next to team names follow it
- Standings screens after each league, every day but only before that league's first game starts or after its last game is final (never mid-games): NFL, NBA and NHL by division (a screen per conference); college football for the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12, ACC, Pac-12, Mountain West and American (college basketball keeps just its Top 25 screen). `?standings=0` turns them off
- March Madness: the NCAAB tab shows the bracket in place of the Top 25 screen. Through the Sweet 16, one screen per region (16 teams down to the region winner) with a line through every eliminated team; once it is down to the last 8, one screen with the Elite Eight, Final Four, championship and champion. `?bracket=0` turns it off
- `?cfb=fbs` every FBS college football game instead of just ranked-team games
- `?rotate=20` seconds per screen
- Colors follow game days (`auto`): on days the Bears, USC or Wisconsin play, the whole screen goes to their colors. USC and Wisconsin keep theirs the whole game day (midnight to midnight, or an hour after the final if it runs late); the Bears from the morning until an hour after the final. USC and Wisconsin on the same day: always USC. Two others on one day: the game on now, then the next to start, then the one that just ended. After the Super Bowl, the Bulls or White Sox colors on their game days. Every other day: the house colors, navy and gold with the Bears orange lines under the header and above the bottom bar. `?theme=house`, `bears`, `usc`, `wis`, `sox`, `bulls` or `classic` (navy and gold, no orange) force one
- `?stats=0` / `?news=0` turn off key stats or headlines in the bottom bar
- Fits any screen: sideways screens from 5:4 to 21:9 fill the screen; anything else (phone or tablet held upright, super-wide monitors) runs in a correctly shaped frame centered on the screen. Phones with rounded corners or a notch keep clear of them automatically. `?inset=3` adds a 3% safety margin on every side for TVs that crop their edges
- `?demo=1` fake games to test the layout
